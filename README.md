# roguelike

A roguelike (Caves of Qud-inspired) in a terminal-looking window, structured so the
rendering/simulation seam is enforced by the compiler rather than by
convention.

## Structure

This is a Cargo workspace with four crates:

- `game/` — a library with all simulation and game logic. It has **no**
  dependency on egui, a terminal crate, or any other graphics crate —
  that's enforced by `game/Cargo.toml` simply never listing one. It exposes:
  - `Cell { glyph, fg, bg }` (with its own `Rgb` type) — the only rendering
    output: `render_view` returns a grid of these exactly the size of the
    frontend's viewport, seen through a camera that follows the player.
  - `InputEvent` (`Move(Dir)`, `Wait`, `PickUp`, `Explore`, `Quit`) — the only input a frontend
    can send in; raw keys never reach `game`.
  - A `GameState` holding an `hecs::World`, the tile map, a message log,
    and seeded RNG streams, mutated only by draining an internal `Command`
    queue rather than directly from input handlers.
  - Origins (`data/origins.ron`, ADR 0025): what a new character starts as,
    in place of a class: a blueprint inheriting the bare `Player` with the
    kit, skills and techniques, and styles known from the start. Abilities
    (`Ability`) come only from an origin, never from a manual or a style.
    Four so far: the wanderer (a bow; the default), the duelist (a rapier,
    fencing followed), the hedge mage (three spells) and the pit fighter
    (fists, Brawling, a headbutt).
  - Camp (`data/camp.ron`, drawn as rows of characters): a new game starts
    there, nothing hostile comes there, and each entrance is stairs down
    into a dungeon. Taking one starts a dive; climbing any floor's stairs
    up comes back to camp and ends it. What you leave on camp's floor is
    still there when you come back.
  - Crafting (`data/properties.ron`, `data/recipes.ron`): creatures leave
    materials (goblin bone, rat hide) and floors hold more (flint, saplings,
    each phase's own); a recipe makes a thing from materials with the
    properties its slots need, and the product takes their properties (a
    cold edge cuts cold). Camp's workbench (`=`) is where most things are
    made; field recipes, like the wanderer's arrows, work anywhere.
  - Death is final: the save is removed and the life joins the lineage
    (`legacy.ron` beside the saves); deleting a living character counts too.
    The next character finds the last lives' camps in ruins, with what
    survived of what they left there (`data/legacy.ron`: how many lives,
    how long ago).
  - Phases (`data/phases.ron`): the world turns through phases. At camp you
    wait for one (a single action); the next dive is in it all the way
    down. A dungeon's spawn entries may belong to some phases only (the
    caves' shamans and extra finds come with the embers), and the phase is
    part of every floor's seed. Each phase but the stillness leans the
    world (ADR 0027): its element hits harder for everyone, it bends one
    rule (light, stamina, missiles, steps), it has its own natives and a
    find, and fighting in it trains its attunement, which softens its
    hardships for you. A style can thrive in a phase, and a discovery can
    need one.
  - Dungeons and floors (ADR 0009, `docs/plans/dives.md`): a floor is a
    160×60 cave, larger than the screen, generated by cellular automata
    from a seed mixed from the world seed, the dungeon, the dive and the
    floor. Each dungeon is data (`data/dungeons/*.ron`): its cave tuning,
    what lives on its floors (more on each floor down) and how many items
    lie about, and how deep it goes (`docs/plans/depth.md`): every third
    floor and the bottom are way-landings, where you arrive on stairs up
    (`<`) back to camp; between them the only way is down, and the bottom
    has no stairs down. You arrive near the centre; the stairs down (`>`)
    sit on the floor farthest from there; creatures start a
    fair distance away, and items (any blueprint with an `Item` part) are
    found by stepping on them. Going down builds a new floor and forgets
    the old one; you bring what you hold and your summons.
  - Inventory and equipment (ADR 0010): carried items are inert; equipped
    ones sit on a body part of the creature's anatomy (`Body`, Qud-style,
    from data) and their parts take effect. `inventory_view` and
    `equipment_view` give frontends plain data, with `ItemRef` handles.
  - Stats and weapons (ADR 0011): creatures have Strength, Agility, Intelligence and Willpower; a
    hit rolls the wielded weapon's dice (or natural melee) plus the
    modifier of the stat that weapon uses. `character_sheet` sums it up.
  - Skills (ADR 0017): flat, overlapping skills from `data/skills.ron`
    (Melee, Dagger, Slash, Dodge, …) grow as the player uses them on
    enemies, less each time against the same kind of foe. A blow's level
    is the weighted average of the skills it uses; it raises hit chance
    and damage. A blow that misses may still graze (half damage), be
    turned aside by a buckler, or be dodged perfectly.
    Casting trains Magic, the school and the spell's form; skill there
    strengthens spells and makes them fizzle less.
    Paying trains the discipline (Channeling, Bloodletting, Scroll lore),
    which makes casts cheaper or scrolls steadier.
  - Discovery (ADR 0018): reaching combinations of skills inspires a
    discovery from `data/discoveries.ron`, granting a proficiency (a part
    attached to you, such as Footwork). Half-formed ideas show in the
    skills panel.
  - Lore (ADR 0017): the first sight of each kind of creature teaches
    Beast lore, which decides what looking (`;`) reveals: health, then
    armor. The first cast of each spell teaches Arcana, which reveals what
    other creatures cast.
  - Training (ADR 0017): manuals teach a chunk of XP once; practice tops
    skills up to a ceiling. Neither inspires a discovery.
  - Styles (ADR 0019): a discovery can grant a style, such as Fencing or
    Brawling. Take it up from the skills panel; it grows with every use
    that follows it, gains principles at levels (both riposte a perfect
    dodge) and invents techniques (ADR 0024).
  - Damage types and armor (ADR 0012): blows are slashing, blunt or
    piercing (a set; punches are blunt), armor resists each kind, and a
    blow of several kinds goes through the weakest spot.
  - Ranged attacks (ADR 0013): throw a `Throwable` or shoot a `Launcher`
    at a tile; the missile flies a straight line to the first creature or
    wall, and meets that creature like a blow (it may miss and drop at its
    feet). Throwing trains Thrown, shooting Archery and Bow. `aim`
    previews a shot for frontends. A quiver holds arrows and
    supplies the bow; a nocked arrow overrides it. The wanderer starts with
    a short bow and 12 arrows, leather, and a torch in the off hand: a held `Light` widens
    the view (8 tiles to 11) and warms the colour of everything it lights.
  - Spells (ADR 0014): a spell is a part attached to its caster; how it is
    paid for, and which stat it scales with, is the caster's discipline for
    its school. Everyone has pyromancy (paid in recharges, scaling with
    Willpower) and arcane magic (mana that regenerates over time, scaling
    with Intelligence); the hedge mage starts knowing firebolt, magic
    missile and the arcane lance (a beam through every foe in line). The
    fireball, a delayed fire rune, chain lightning and a spirit-wolf
    summoning exist for styles to invent.
    Casting runs `ModifyCast` (a worn focus), `CheckCast` (the spell, then
    the caster), `CastRisk` (armor makes casting fizzle) and `PayCost`.
    Parts make a spell an area (burst, cone, beam, nova), a chain, a
    delayed rune or a summoning; spells deal fire, cold or shock, resisted
    per element. Scrolls cast themselves and crumble. Goblin shamans cast
    blood bolts with their own health. `spells` lists what the player can
    cast, with what each would cost.
  - Techniques (ADR 0024): special actions attached like spells but paid
    in turns and recharges (and stamina, dormant: every cost is 0), never
    through disciplines. Their blows are ordinary blows, so they land,
    graze and train like a bump. `Need`s gate them on what is wielded or a
    skill's level. Area techniques cover ground as spells of their shape do.
    Riders do more on a full hit: a shove, or a timed effect.
    Every origin starts knowing the trip (the pit fighter its headbutt too);
    manuals teach techniques when studied
    (`Teaches`), inspiration can bring one (the cleave), and a followed
    style invents its own at levels of its skill (Fencing the lunge,
    Brawling the shove), only in a real fight.
  - Sides and summons (ADR 0015): hostiles and the player's side, a summon
    on its owner's. AI goes for the nearest enemy it perceives; summons
    last until their binding runs out or their owner dies.
  - Auto-explore: `explore_step` walks the player, one turn at a time,
    to the nearest unfound item they know of, then to unexplored ground,
    then to the exit, stopping as soon as a hostile is in view.
  - `Blueprints`, loaded from `data/entities/*.ron` (one blueprint per
    file, named by file stem) via serde + RON. Blueprints list
    components, can `inherits` from a parent (resolved at load time), and
    can carry `equipment` spawned as attached entities. Every name a
    blueprint refers to is validated and resolved to a `BlueprintId` at
    load; a missing one fails loading. Sprites a blueprint's `Appearance`
    names come from `data/tiles.ron` (ADR 0023), checked the same way.
  - On-hit effects: an `OnHit` part on a creature or its equipment rolls
    to apply effects after each melee hit that deals damage. Goblins
    carry a rusty knife that makes the victim bleed a quarter of the time.
  - An `EventRegistry` of typed handlers per (event, component). Handlers
    read the world, rewrite or stop the event, and emit commands; only
    `apply_commands` mutates the world.
  - Game time (ADR 0006): a global tick clock, energy-based turn order
    by `Speed`, a timer queue, and timed effects (e.g. bleeding) whose
    durations are stored relative to their start. `take_turn` applies
    the player's action and runs the world until the player is ready
    again. Hostile creatures (goblins) chase and attack the nearest enemy
    they can see: the player or the player's summons.
  - Field of view (ADR 0007): symmetric shadowcasting with a circular
    radius, recomputed each player turn. The view shows what the player
    sees; terrain seen before stays on screen dimmed, without creatures.
  - Save games (ADR 0008): `save::to_ron` / `save::from_ron` turn a
    `GameState` into a versioned RON string and back. `game` does no file
    IO; saving is only allowed between player turns.
- `front/` — a library every frontend shares (ADR 0020): a `Session` that
  turns already-mapped input into turns and mode changes (play, the
  hovering panel, choosers, the aiming and look cursors, auto-explore and
  practice runs), the status text, the title menu, and save files: one per
  character, loaded from the menu and written (atomically) on quit. No
  drawing and no key bindings.
- `gui/` — the windowed frontend (eframe/egui, ADR 0020), on the same
  `front` crate with the same keys: the title menu, then the map with a
  side column (character sheet, equipment) and a strip for the log and
  status. Run it with `cargo run -p gui -- --seed 42`. It looks like the
  terminal, in one bundled font (DejaVu Sans Mono, licence in
  `gui/assets`), and animates hits, misses, deaths and flights
  (`game::fx`). Its build plan was `docs/plans/gui.md`. (A terminal
  frontend, `term`, came first; it was retired once the window had
  everything, and lives on in git history.)
- `bench/` — the balance bench (`docs/plans/balance.md`), a tool, not part
  of the game: builds (`bench/builds/*.ron`) fight training dolls
  (`bench/scenarios.ron`) through the real game (`game::balance`) over many
  seeds. `cargo balance` writes the report (`bench/report.md`, and its
  numbers as `bench/report.json`) and says what moved; `--check` only
  compares; `--build`, `--scenario`, `--dive` narrow a run to a question;
  `--page FILE` writes a readable page with charts and what moved since the
  committed report.

## Controls

Title menu (New game, Continue, Load, Fallen):

- Arrow keys or `j`/`k`: select; `Enter`: choose; `Esc` or `q`: back/quit.
- One character lives at a time: New game is offered once the last one has
  died (or been deleted, which counts as a death).
- New game asks for a character name (letters, digits, `-`, `_`), then
  an origin: what the character starts as, with its kit, skills, techniques,
  spells and styles listed under it; `Esc` goes back to the name. A save
  from an older save format can't load; a new game with its name replaces
  it (Load marks such saves).
- Continue resumes the most recently saved character.
- Load lists saves, newest first: `Enter` loads, `r` renames, `d` deletes
  (with confirmation).
- Fallen lists the lineage's past lives, newest first: who they were, how
  they ended and their best skills.
- On death a panel says how the life ended; any key returns to the title.

In game:

- Arrow keys or `hjkl`: move orthogonally.
- `y` / `u` / `b` / `n`: move diagonally (NW/NE/SW/SE). A step onto a
  tile marked for a coming blow is refused once with a warning; the same
  step again goes. A step onto rubble (`,`) costs two turns; deep water
  (`~`) and chasms (`:`) are never walked, but a shove can put something in.
- `.`: wait a turn.
- `g` or `,`: pick up everything on your tile (one turn; nothing there costs
  nothing).
- `x`: auto-explore: find every item, explore the floor, then walk to the
  stairs down. Stops when a hostile comes into view; any key interrupts it.
- `>`: take the stairs down: from camp, into the dungeon that entrance leads
  to; on a floor, to the next one. You bring what you hold and your
  summons; the floor you leave is gone.
- `<`: climb the stairs up, back to camp (only on a way-landing: every third
  floor and the bottom; the side panel says where the next one is). The
  dive ends there.
- `W` (at camp, in the window): choose the phase to wait for; the next dive
  is in it. Under the list, what the selected phase leans and bends.
- In the window (`gui`), the mouse is a shortcut onto the same keys:
  hovering the map moves the look or aim cursor, a click goes there
  (travel), fires, or picks a list row, a right click cancels aiming,
  hovering in play shows what looking would, and a right click on a panel
  row lists what you can do with it. `F2` switches between glyphs and
  sprites (`data/tiles.ron`; the shipped atlas is a placeholder).
- `;`: look around: a cursor (`Tab` jumps between creatures in view) and
  the status bar says what is there, as much as your Beast lore reads.
  `Enter` walks there over ground you know, a step at a time, stopping
  when a hostile comes into view (any key interrupts it); `Esc` stops
  looking.
- `p`: practise: drill with what you wield, a turn at a time, until you
  can learn no more alone (half your best level in that domain) or an
  enemy comes into view; any key interrupts it.
- `C`: close an open door beside you (walking into a closed door opens
  it, a turn). A closed door blocks sight; the castle has them in most
  doorways.
- `Z`: start or stop sprinting: a step costs half a turn and a point of
  stamina (ten to start, a point back every two turns), and you can't
  attack while sprinting. It ends when your breath runs out.
- `?`: show why the latest log lines happened (a miss's odds and what made
  them) under each, until the next key.
- `R`: rest: wait a turn at a time while health comes back (a point every
  three turns), until healed, the floor's rest budget is spent (the status
  line's `Rest:`) or an enemy comes into view; any key interrupts it. Camp
  heals in full.
- `i` / `e` / `s` / `S`: the inventory / equipment / skills / styles panel
  over the map. `Tab`/`Shift-Tab` cycle views, `j`/`k` or arrows select (long lists
  scroll), `Esc`/`q` closes (pressing the open view's key again also
  closes).
  - Inventory: `Enter` equips on the first part that fits, `d` drops.
    On an arrow: `n` nocks it on your bow (a turn, and it overrides the
    quiver: use it for special arrows), `v` puts it in your quiver.
    Item rows say which skills using the item trains.
    On a manual: `r` studies it (once per kind of manual; it takes a while).
    Some manuals teach techniques: the lunge, the sweep, the tri-sword.
  - Equipment: one row per body part. `Enter` on a filled part unequips;
    on an empty one it lists what you carry that fits, and `Enter` equips
    it there (`Esc` backs out).
  - Skills: what you have learned, by domain, with each level and the XP
    toward the next.
  - Styles: your styles (`Enter` takes one up or sets it aside, a turn),
    proficiencies, techniques and half-formed ideas. The
    side panel shows the style you follow.
  - Equipping, unequipping and dropping take a turn each; the panel stays
    open.
- `t`: throw something (carried or equipped; one turn). A list appears if
  you have several throwables. A cursor starts on the nearest hostile in
  view: `Tab`/`Shift-Tab` cycle targets, movement keys nudge it, `Enter`
  or `Space` throws, `Esc`/`q` cancels. The path is marked and the status
  bar shows the damage after armor; the missile hits the first creature on
  the line and lands there (or before a wall), ready to pick up again.
  The missile is animated flying; any key skips it.
- `f`: fire the bow you wield, with the nocked arrow, else the next one
  from your quiver; aiming works as for `t`. With neither, a loose arrow
  in your pack is nocked first (one turn), then `f` again aims. Arrows that
  miss land intact; ones that hit may break. Arrows you pick up fill your
  quiver first.
- `c`: cast a spell you know (one turn). A list shows each spell's range,
  shape, damage and price (firebolt: a 6-turn recharge; magic missile: 3
  mana, shown as MP in the side panel with how fast it comes back:
  Intelligence mostly deepens the pool, Willpower mostly quickens it), and why it can't be cast now, if
  so; `Enter` picks one and aiming works as for `t`. An area spell marks
  the ground it would cover and the status bar says how many it catches:
  a burst is aimed at a spot, a beam or cone in a direction. A fireball
  burns you too if you stand in it. Elemental spells are resisted by the
  matching resistance (shown as Resist in the side panel), not by armor;
  wearing armor gives a chance to fizzle, shown in the list. Scrolls you
  carry are listed too and crumble once read. A summoning shows where its
  creatures will appear; walking into your own summon swaps places.
- `a`: perform a technique you know (one turn). A list shows each one's
  reach, damage and recharge, and why it can't be performed now, if so (the
  lunge needs a thrusting weapon); `Enter` picks one and aiming works as for
  `t`. The cursor only stops on enemies the technique can reach in a
  straight line, and the status bar shows the damage after armor and the
  chances. A lunge steps in from two tiles away and strikes; a cleave strikes
  every enemy around you, a sweep those in a cone in front (both need an
  edge, such as a sabre), each blow rolled on its own. A shove (free off
  hand) drives an enemy back two tiles; a trip leaves it reeling: easier to
  hit and unable to dodge perfectly, for three turns. A followed style can
  strengthen a technique (the list says "with Fencing"): fencers lunge more
  often from Fencing 2, and from 5 their lunge leaves the target reeling.
  The tri-sword is a form of three sword techniques in turn (horizontal
  slash, vertical slash, stab), listed as one row that becomes the next
  step: each step must follow the one before within two turns, hits harder
  than the last, and the run's blows teach more the longer it goes.
  Goblins shove you back now and then; spirit wolves trip.
- `X`: cycle what auto-explore does at items: note them and walk on
  (default), stop on them, or pick them up. Saved with the character.
- `q` or `Esc`: save and return to the title menu.
- After you die, any key returns to the title without saving; the last save
  is kept.

Moving, attacking (by moving into a creature), bumping a wall and waiting
each take one turn. The status bar shows where you are and the current
turn. A side column shows where you are and what comes next, the character
(HP, stats, armor, damage and its kinds, weapon, auto-explore setting) and
what each body part holds.

## Running

```bash
nix develop --command cargo run -p gui
# or, with a fixed seed:
nix develop --command cargo run -p gui -- --seed 42
```

Each character has one save, `<saves>/<name>.ron`. The game opens on the
title menu; `--character <name>` skips it and plays that character
straight away (quitting still returns to the menu). `--saves <dir>`
overrides the platform data directory. `--seed` applies to new games only.
A save that fails to load is left untouched and the error is shown in the
menu.

With direnv installed and `direnv allow`'d in this directory, the `nix
develop` shell activates automatically and you can just run `cargo run -p
gui`.

## Notes on the rendering seam

- The tile↔pixel mapping lives in one place, `gui::map_view` (`TILE`,
  `Layout`, `viewport_tiles`); which world tiles the viewport shows is
  `game`'s business (`game::camera`).
