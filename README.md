# roguelike

A terminal roguelike skeleton (Caves of Qud-inspired), structured so the
rendering/simulation seam is enforced by the compiler rather than by
convention.

## Structure

This is a Cargo workspace with two crates:

- `game/` — a library with all simulation and game logic. It has **no**
  dependency on crossterm, ratatui, or any other terminal/graphics crate —
  that's enforced by `game/Cargo.toml` simply never listing one. It exposes:
  - `Cell { glyph, fg, bg }` (with its own `Rgb` type) — the only rendering
    output: `render_view` returns a grid of these exactly the size of the
    frontend's viewport, seen through a camera that follows the player.
  - `InputEvent` (`Move(Dir)`, `Wait`, `PickUp`, `Explore`, `Quit`) — the only input a frontend
    can send in; raw keys never reach `game`.
  - A `GameState` holding an `hecs::World`, the tile map, a message log,
    and seeded RNG streams, mutated only by draining an internal `Command`
    queue rather than directly from input handlers.
  - Zones (ADR 0009): the world is one 160×60 cave zone, larger than the
    screen, generated by cellular automata from a seed derived from the
    world seed and the zone's coordinates. Tuning lives in
    `data/cave.ron`. The player starts near the centre; goblins start a
    fair distance away. The exit (`>`) sits on the floor farthest from the
    start, and a few items (any blueprint with an `Item` part) lie on the
    floor; stepping on one finds it.
  - Inventory and equipment (ADR 0010): carried items are inert; equipped
    ones sit on a body part of the creature's anatomy (`Body`, Qud-style,
    from data) and their parts take effect. `inventory_view` and
    `equipment_view` give frontends plain data, with `ItemRef` handles.
  - Stats and weapons (ADR 0011): creatures have Strength, Agility, Intelligence and Willpower; a
    hit rolls the wielded weapon's dice (or natural melee) plus the
    modifier of the stat that weapon uses. `character_sheet` sums it up.
  - Skills (ADR 0017): flat, overlapping skills from `data/skills.ron`
    (Melee, Dagger, Slash, Dodge, …) grow as the player uses them on
    enemies, less each time against the same kind of foe. A blow's level
    is the weighted average of the skills it uses; it raises hit chance
    and damage. A blow that misses may still graze (half damage), be
    turned aside by a buckler, or be dodged perfectly.
    Casting trains Magic, the school and the spell's form; skill there
    strengthens spells and makes them fizzle less.
  - Discovery (ADR 0018): reaching combinations of skills inspires a
    discovery from `data/discoveries.ron`, granting a proficiency (a part
    attached to you, such as Footwork). Half-formed ideas show in the
    skills panel.
  - Styles (ADR 0019): a discovery can grant a style, such as Fencing. Take
    it up from the skills panel; it grows with every use that follows it
    and gains principles at levels (Fencing ripostes a perfect dodge).
  - Damage types and armor (ADR 0012): blows are slashing, blunt or
    piercing (a set; punches are blunt), armor resists each kind, and a
    blow of several kinds goes through the weakest spot.
  - Ranged attacks (ADR 0013): throw a `Throwable` or shoot a `Launcher`
    at a tile; the missile flies a straight line to the first creature or
    wall, and meets that creature like a blow (it may miss and drop at its
    feet). Throwing trains Thrown, shooting Archery and Bow. `aim`
    previews a shot for frontends. A quiver holds arrows and
    supplies the bow; a nocked arrow overrides it. You start with a short
    bow and 12 arrows.
  - Spells (ADR 0014): a spell is a part attached to its caster; how it is
    paid for, and which stat it scales with, is the caster's discipline for
    its school. You start with pyromancy (firebolt, and the fireball that
    bursts where it lands: a recharge, scales with Willpower) and arcane
    magic (magic missile, and the arcane lance, a beam through every foe in
    line: mana that regenerates over time, scales with Intelligence),
    plus a delayed fire rune, chain lightning and a spirit-wolf summoning.
    Casting runs `ModifyCast` (a worn focus), `CheckCast` (the spell, then
    the caster), `CastRisk` (armor makes casting fizzle) and `PayCost`.
    Parts make a spell an area (burst, cone, beam, nova), a chain, a
    delayed rune or a summoning; spells deal fire, cold or shock, resisted
    per element. Scrolls cast themselves and crumble. Goblin shamans cast
    blood bolts with their own health. `spells` lists what the player can
    cast, with what each would cost.
  - Sides and summons (ADR 0015): hostiles and the player's side, a summon
    on its owner's. AI goes for the nearest enemy it perceives; summons
    last until their binding runs out or their owner dies.
  - Auto-explore: `explore_step` walks the player, one turn at a time,
    to the nearest unfound item they know of, then to unexplored ground,
    then to the exit, stopping as soon as a hostile is in view.
  - `Blueprints`, loaded from `data/entities/*.ron` (one blueprint per
    file, named by file stem) via serde + RON. Blueprints list
    components, can `inherits` from a parent (resolved at load time), and
    can carry `equipment` spawned as attached entities. Every name a
    blueprint refers to is validated and resolved to a `BlueprintId` at
    load; a missing one fails loading.
  - On-hit effects: an `OnHit` part on a creature or its equipment rolls
    to apply effects after each melee hit that deals damage. Goblins
    carry a rusty knife that makes the victim bleed a quarter of the time.
  - An `EventRegistry` of typed handlers per (event, component). Handlers
    read the world, rewrite or stop the event, and emit commands; only
    `apply_commands` mutates the world.
  - Game time (ADR 0006): a global tick clock, energy-based turn order
    by `Speed`, a timer queue, and timed effects (e.g. bleeding) whose
    durations are stored relative to their start. `take_turn` applies
    the player's action and runs the world until the player is ready
    again. Hostile creatures (goblins) chase and attack the nearest enemy
    they can see: the player or the player's summons.
  - Field of view (ADR 0007): symmetric shadowcasting with a circular
    radius, recomputed each player turn. The view shows what the player
    sees; terrain seen before stays on screen dimmed, without creatures.
  - Save games (ADR 0008): `save::to_ron` / `save::from_ron` turn a
    `GameState` into a versioned RON string and back. `game` does no file
    IO; saving is only allowed between player turns.
- `term/` — the binary frontend: crossterm (reached only through
  `ratatui::crossterm`, never as a direct dependency) + ratatui. It maps
  raw key events to `game::InputEvent`, renders `Grid<Cell>` through a
  clipped custom `MapWidget`, and owns all terminal lifecycle concerns
  (raw mode, alternate screen, Kitty keyboard protocol enhancement when
  supported, panic-safe restore). Its UI is the map, log and status, a
  side column of panels (hidden under 80 columns), and a hovering panel for
  inventory and equipment, all drawn from `game`'s views. It also owns the title menu and save
  files: one per character, loaded from the menu and written (atomically)
  on quit.

A future windowed/tileset frontend would be a new crate depending on
`game`, with no changes to `game` itself.

## Controls

Title menu (New game, Continue, Load):

- Arrow keys or `j`/`k`: select; `Enter`: choose; `Esc` or `q`: back/quit.
- New game asks for a character name (letters, digits, `-`, `_`).
- Continue resumes the most recently saved character.
- Load lists saves, newest first: `Enter` loads, `r` renames, `d` deletes
  (with confirmation).

In game:

- Arrow keys or `hjkl`: move orthogonally.
- `y` / `u` / `b` / `n`: move diagonally (NW/NE/SW/SE).
- `.`: wait a turn.
- `g` or `,`: pick up everything on your tile (one turn; nothing there costs
  nothing).
- `x`: auto-explore: find every item, explore the zone, then walk to the
  exit. Stops when a hostile comes into view; any key interrupts it.
- `i` / `e` / `s`: the inventory / equipment / skills panel over the map.
  `Tab` cycles views, `j`/`k` or arrows select, `Esc`/`q` closes (pressing the open
  view's key again also closes).
  - Inventory: `Enter` equips on the first part that fits, `d` drops.
    On an arrow: `n` nocks it on your bow (a turn, and it overrides the
    quiver: use it for special arrows), `v` puts it in your quiver.
    Item rows say which skills using the item trains.
  - Equipment: one row per body part. `Enter` on a filled part unequips;
    on an empty one it lists what you carry that fits, and `Enter` equips
    it there (`Esc` backs out).
  - Skills: what you have learned, by domain, with each level and the XP
    toward the next; then your styles (`Enter` takes one up or sets it
    aside, a turn), proficiencies and half-formed ideas.
  - Equipping, unequipping and dropping take a turn each; the panel stays
    open.
- `t`: throw something (carried or equipped; one turn). A list appears if
  you have several throwables. A cursor starts on the nearest hostile in
  view: `Tab`/`Shift-Tab` cycle targets, movement keys nudge it, `Enter`
  or `Space` throws, `Esc`/`q` cancels. The path is marked and the status
  bar shows the damage after armor; the missile hits the first creature on
  the line and lands there (or before a wall), ready to pick up again.
  The missile is animated flying; any key skips it.
- `f`: fire the bow you wield, with the nocked arrow, else the next one
  from your quiver; aiming works as for `t`. With neither, a loose arrow
  in your pack is nocked first (one turn), then `f` again aims. Arrows that
  miss land intact; ones that hit may break. Arrows you pick up fill your
  quiver first.
- `c`: cast a spell you know (one turn). A list shows each spell's range,
  shape, damage and price (firebolt: a 6-turn recharge; magic missile: 3
  mana, shown as MP in the side panel with how fast it comes back:
  Intelligence mostly deepens the pool, Willpower mostly quickens it), and why it can't be cast now, if
  so; `Enter` picks one and aiming works as for `t`. An area spell marks
  the ground it would cover and the status bar says how many it catches:
  a burst is aimed at a spot, a beam or cone in a direction. A fireball
  burns you too if you stand in it. Elemental spells are resisted by the
  matching resistance (shown as Resist in the side panel), not by armor;
  wearing armor gives a chance to fizzle, shown in the list. Scrolls you
  carry are listed too and crumble once read. A summoning shows where its
  creatures will appear; walking into your own summon swaps places.
- `X`: cycle what auto-explore does at items: note them and walk on
  (default), stop on them, or pick them up. Saved with the character.
- `q` or `Esc`: save and return to the title menu.
- After you die, any key returns to the title without saving; the last save
  is kept.

Moving, attacking (by moving into a creature), bumping a wall and waiting
each take one turn. The status bar shows the current turn. On terminals at least 80
columns wide, a side column shows the character (HP, stats, armor,
damage and its kinds, weapon, auto-explore setting) and what each body part holds.

## Running

```bash
nix develop --command cargo run -p term
# or, with a fixed seed:
nix develop --command cargo run -p term -- --seed 42
```

Each character has one save, `<saves>/<name>.ron`. The game opens on the
title menu; `--character <name>` skips it and plays that character
straight away (quitting still returns to the menu). `--saves <dir>`
overrides the platform data directory. `--seed` applies to new games only.
A save that fails to load is left untouched and the error is shown in the
menu.

With direnv installed and `direnv allow`'d in this directory, the `nix
develop` shell activates automatically and you can just run `cargo run -p
term`.

## Notes on the rendering seam

- One map tile currently renders as one terminal character. The
  tile-to-screen mapping lives in a single function,
  `term::widgets::map_widget::tile_to_screen` (with `viewport_tiles`, its
  inverse for the map panel's size), so switching to two characters per
  tile (terminal cells are roughly 1:2, width:height) later means editing
  only those. Which world tiles the viewport shows is `game`'s business
  (`game::camera`).
